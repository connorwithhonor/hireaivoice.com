AI receptionist for the calls your team misses

An AI receptionist answers forwarded business calls, identifies itself as AI, and works from information you approve. It can ask what the caller needs, collect callback details, and explain the next step. People handle judgment, exceptions, and quotes. Appointment confirmation depends on a connected calendar that has passed testing.

What does the caller hear when the phone rolls over?

Picture a service owner finishing a job in Santa Clarita. The phone rings while their hands are full. In an agreed overflow setup, the unanswered call reaches the AI receptionist. It names the business and makes its AI identity clear before asking how it can help.

Suppose the caller wants someone to look at a noisy garage door. The receptionist asks for the service location, the reason for calling, and a callback number. It can use approved information to explain whether the business handles garage doors. It does not decide which part has failed or tell the caller how to repair it.

The caller might change the request halfway through. Maybe the door is at a rental property and the person calling is not on site. That distinction belongs in the handoff. A useful conversation leaves the office with an accurate request, including what still needs clarification.

What information do I need to give it?

Start with the questions your desk already answers. Which services do you provide? Where do you work? When is the office open? Who receives a new inquiry, and who handles an existing customer? Write down the exceptions as carefully as the normal route.

Give the AI receptionist a small set of approved answers. A service list is useful. A collection of outdated notes with conflicting hours is not. Assign a person to maintain those facts when the business changes. If an answer is missing, the call should become a question for staff.

The setup also needs a closing sentence that matches your operation. If the office reviews requests after reopening, say that. Do not suggest a technician is on the way just because a message has been captured. The words at the end of the call shape what the customer expects next.

What gets handed to a human?

The person following up needs more than a notification that a call happened. They need the caller's stated problem, location, contact details, preferred timing, and any unanswered question. The record should distinguish the caller's description from something the business has actually confirmed.

A request for a person should follow an agreed route. That may be a transfer during staffed hours or a callback request when the office is closed. Test what happens if the transfer destination does not answer. A ringing phone at the other end does not prove that someone took responsibility.

For the garage door example, the office decides whether an inspection is appropriate and who should call back. A staff member owns that decision. The AI receptionist should preserve uncertainty rather than convert a customer's guess about the problem into a diagnosis.

What will the AI receptionist never decide?

The approved scope excludes diagnosis, quotes, legal advice, and medical advice. It does not invent a repair price, assess symptoms, interpret a contract, or promise a result. Those requests go to a qualified person through the agreed process. Routine intake is not emergency dispatch.

This boundary matters even when the question sounds simple. A caller asking whether a noise means the motor needs replacing is asking for a technical conclusion. A caller asking whether the company works on garage doors is asking about an approved service. The receptionist can handle the service question and pass the technical question onward.

Also keep payment information, access codes, and detailed sensitive records out of ordinary intake. Before activation, review the actual recording, transcription, summary, and retention settings. Explain what is processed and stored. Turning off an audio recording alone does not describe the whole data path.

Can it put someone on my calendar?

Capturing a requested time is different from reserving it. The receptionist can note that the caller prefers an afternoon visit. Your team then checks the request and confirms what is available. That is a complete, useful workflow even without direct booking.

Direct booking belongs in the scope only when the supported calendar connection works under your real rules. Existing appointments, working hours, job length, and unavailable staff all affect whether a time can be offered. The caller should hear confirmation only after a reservation succeeds.

Test a disconnected calendar as well as a working one. When the connection fails, the fallback should collect a preference and explain that staff must confirm. It should never turn a failed calendar action into a spoken promise.

How do we test it before launch?

Use the public demo to hear the conversation style. Interrupt it, correct a detail, and ask for a human. Then bring your business rules to Connor. The demo is a listening exercise; your configured business flow needs its own acceptance calls.

Run those calls through the intended forwarding route before customer traffic is enabled. Try a routine inquiry, an unsupported service, a changed callback number, and a transfer nobody answers. Ask for a quote and a diagnosis to check the boundary. Inspect what arrives for the staff member assigned to respond.

Keep a written record of what the caller heard and what the team received. Fix unclear answers and repeat the affected cases. Agree on how to restore the prior phone route if the new flow fails. Launch readiness is a usable handoff, not just a pleasant voice.

How does this compare with voicemail or a live service?

Voicemail records what a caller chooses to leave. It cannot ask for a missing location or clarify whether the request concerns an existing job. It may still be enough for an office that returns messages reliably and does not need intake questions answered during the call.

A live answering service puts a person into the conversation. Its scope may include message taking, transfers, or scheduling. Ask what the actual service handles. A human can be the better fit when callers need discretion, reassurance, or a conversation that cannot stay within a narrow set of rules.

What else should I know before choosing an AI receptionist?

Will callers be told they are speaking with AI?

Yes. The greeting identifies the AI receptionist. Test that opening on the configured business line so disclosure remains clear after forwarding. The voice should never present itself as a human employee or use a confusing introduction.

Do I have to replace my current phone number?

The starting point is your existing business line. Connor checks whether its carrier supports the forwarding route you need. Keep the current arrangement available until that route and the return path have been tested.

What if the caller refuses to give a detail?

The receptionist should record what the caller chooses to provide and identify what is missing. It should not invent the answer. Staff can decide whether the available information is enough for a useful callback.

Does an answered call count as a booked job?

No. A conversation, a captured request, an appointment, and completed work are different outcomes. Review what happened after the handoff so your records show the actual result rather than treating every answered call as revenue.

Who changes the answers when my business changes?

Name an owner for the approved business information. When hours, services, or routing change, update the relevant answers and repeat the affected call tests. Old instructions can cause problems even when the phone connection still works.
