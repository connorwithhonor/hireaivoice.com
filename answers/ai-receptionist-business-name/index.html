What should the caller hear at the beginning?

I give the opening two jobs. Say your business name. Tell the caller AI answered. Both belong before the questions start. HireAIVoice makes that disclosure at the beginning. Someone calling your number should not have to stop the conversation to figure out who answered or whether they reached the right place.

Tell me how you greet customers now, which name they recognize, and what tone fits your work. I want enough introduction to orient the caller, then room for the request. Read the proposed greeting as someone hearing your business name for the first time. If promotional wording buries the name or delays the question, I would cut it. A caller looking for your hours should not have to sit through a pitch to get them.

What business information do I provide?

I need more than the name on your sign. Give me the services the assistant may discuss, your hours, your service area, common questions, and booking rules. Those are the facts behind the greeting. Once your name is spoken, the rest of the call has to represent the same business.

Tell me which work you turn down, too. I do not want an accurate greeting followed by a promise you cannot keep. Brief the assistant as you would someone about to answer your phone: here is what callers need, here is what you can accept, and here is where a person must take over.

Can the greeting imply a human receptionist answered?

No. I keep the AI identification in the opening. Your business name does not give the assistant permission to impersonate you or someone on your staff. Listen as a new caller would. If the wording suggests an actual employee picked up, that wording needs attention before it reaches your customers.

I judge voice quality and identity separately. A natural voice still has to say it is AI. Once the caller knows who answered, the conversation can move into your approved questions. There is no need to invent a staff member to represent your business. The service information has to carry the call.

How do I check that it sounds right for us?

I want you to hear the entire opening in a practice call. Can you recognize your business name? Is the AI disclosure clear? Is there room to explain why you called? Ask me to demonstrate the proposed setup. Those are checks to perform, not promises about a particular voice or pronunciation setting.

Then keep talking. Ask about a service you offer and follow with an exception. I want to hear accurate information and see a handoff someone can use. A welcoming first sentence earns very little if the next answer makes a commitment your business cannot deliver. That is why I review the conversation past the greeting.

What happens when our hours or services change?

When your hours, staffing, services, or phone settings change, I want the approved information and routing reviewed together. A greeting that mentions availability needs to describe today's arrangement. It cannot keep announcing an old schedule while the rest of your operation has moved on. The name may stay the same; the instructions may not.

Choose who will bring those changes into the call-flow review. Then we test the affected conversation and handoff. If your seasonal hours change, I want to hear what an after-hours caller gets told. That check gives you something concrete to approve: wording that matches when your business can respond and what your team can deliver.

Can we omit the AI disclosure?

No. I keep the AI disclosure at the beginning of the HireAIVoice conversation so callers know what answered.

I would identify your business and the AI role. The assistant should not pretend to be an actual member of your staff.

Is approving the greeting enough?

No. I also review service answers, restrictions, and the handoff with you. Your business is represented by the whole call, including what gets promised after the greeting.
