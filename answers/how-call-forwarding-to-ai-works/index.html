How does call forwarding to an AI receptionist work?

I want your existing number to keep doing its job. Call forwarding sends selected calls to a dedicated AI destination. HireAIVoice checks and tests carrier support for the agreed busy or no-answer route while keeping your team involved. We confirm coverage hours, voicemail, and rollback so you know where calls go.

Will customers need a new number?

I start with the number your customers already dial. They keep using it. The change is the route a call takes when the agreed condition applies. Before discussing the AI greeting, I need to understand the line you have now. Otherwise, we are designing an opening without knowing how callers will reach it.

Show me whether your line reaches a desk, cell phone, voicemail, or answering service. Tell me about forwarding already in place. We need the order of those steps before adding a destination. I also want the person who handles missed calls involved. They may know about a voicemail or handoff that is missing from the diagram. Bring that full arrangement to the fit call so we can review the route callers actually use.

Which calls should go to the assistant?

I begin with conditional forwarding and whoever manages your phone. A tested no-answer route lets your business ring first. A busy route covers calls arriving while the line is in use. Your carrier, account, and current settings determine what is available and how it behaves. We check those details before depending on either route.

We also agree on when coverage applies. After-hours and overflow describe what your business needs; forwarding settings determine where the call travels. I test how the two work together. Selecting no-answer forwarding does not, by itself, establish every schedule or fallback. The call needs to follow the coverage arrangement you actually approved.

Can I use the codes in the forwarding guide?

I treat the forwarding guide's published codes as examples to confirm with your provider. Plans and devices can differ. Use the dedicated answering destination assigned to your business during setup. The public demo lets you hear the conversation. It does not replace the check that establishes where your own business calls should be forwarded.

Bring in whoever controls the phone account. I want that person to understand the current settings, the proposed change, and the verified way to undo it. Guessing at codes while customers call is a poor test. A checked procedure gives you something repeatable when staffing or phone arrangements later require a change.

What should the test include?

I test from the business number. Does your team get its expected chance to answer? Leave the call unanswered and check the destination. If busy forwarding is included, test that condition separately. We also review what voicemail does. I need to observe the route rather than assume the carrier handles each situation as intended.

Stay on the call after the greeting. Ask a routine question and leave a request. I want to see usable details reach the right person. Then examine the agreed behavior when the coverage destination is unavailable. The test has to cover the telephone path and the next action your business needs to take.

How do I return to the previous arrangement?

I want rollback instructions verified during testing and kept with the person managing your phone. You should know how to restore the previous arrangement before launch. Put that tested procedure beside the approved call-flow notes. If a problem appears, the phone owner needs instructions they can use, not another routing question to solve.

Start with limited coverage and inspect the actual requests before expanding it. When hours, staffing, or phone settings change, I want the affected route rechecked. The first successful call is only evidence for that arrangement. Knowing who owns forwarding and how to reverse it gives you a usable way to manage later adjustments.
