How do I test an AI receptionist before I switch it on?

I do not approve a business phone setup because the demo sounded good. Start there, then test your configured HireAIVoice flow: approved answers, carrier routing, interruptions, failed transfers, and unavailable calendars. I inspect what reaches your team, correct failures, and verify rollback before limited coverage. You need evidence from your own setup.

What can the public demo tell me?

Use the demo to hear the conversation for yourself. Explain your business, ask a relevant question, and request a person. I want you coming to the fit call with something you heard and can discuss. That is the demo's job: give you a concrete starting point for deciding what your own setup needs.

I keep that listening exercise separate from approving your business route. The demo cannot prove your carrier forwards properly, your receiving phone takes a transfer, or your calendar reserves a time. We have to test the configured route with the people who will use it. My LAPD Academy instructor background taught me to check what works under pressure.

What should I prepare for a business test?

Write down your services, hours, service area, approved answers, and exclusions. Include booking behavior and who handles exceptions. I need those instructions in front of the reviewer before the test call. Approval comes before launch, and the approved wording gives us something to check against. Without an expected outcome, a plausible response can pass for a correct one. I want your business requirement deciding the result, not a favorable impression of the voice.

Choose questions that resemble your actual work. I would include a normal inquiry, work you do not accept, and a caller correcting an earlier detail. Ask a common question several ways. The setup includes varied practice scenarios before the assistant touches your line, followed by your review and whatever adjustments those tests show are needed.

How do I test the telephone route?

Bring whoever manages your business number into the routing test. I check the agreed forwarding condition, your team's opportunity to answer, and voicemail behavior. Use the destination assigned to the business. Carrier examples in the forwarding guide need account-specific confirmation. They are not instructions to apply automatically to every phone that reaches this stage.

I test the way back as well. Keep verified rollback instructions with the phone owner. If live transfer is in scope, try the receiving destination while someone can answer and again while nobody can. We need an accurate fallback for the caller and a usable request for the person who will follow up afterward.

How do I know the handoff actually worked?

I do not stop at the assistant's closing sentence. Open the request with whoever owns the callback. Check the caller's details, need, corrections, and next action. Verify delivery and access. Then have that person accept responsibility and record an outcome. That is how we find out whether the conversation became work your team can finish.

If booking is included, I inspect the calendar. Test unavailable times and a failed connection as well as a successful reservation. If the setup only collects preferences, check the language on both sides. Neither the caller nor your staff should come away believing a requested time is already confirmed when it still needs review.

Can the team finish the request from a failed test call?

I keep a test log: expected result, observed behavior, and the person responsible for correcting a failure. Retest the affected path after the fix. Passing an ordinary inquiry says little about an unavailable recipient or a corrected number. The launch decision needs evidence for the routes your customers are actually going to use.

Begin with the limited coverage window in the agreed plan. I want actual requests and follow-up reviewed before expanding responsibility. That rollout can continue, change, or stop based on what it shows. For you, readiness means the approved workflow has evidence behind it and someone owns the next action after each call.

What else should I check?

Is calling the demo enough to approve launch?

No. I follow the demo with tests of your approved answers, business phone route, handoff, and any connected calendar before approving launch.

Who should review the test request?

I include whoever will return customer calls. That person needs to confirm the request is accessible, accurate, and useful enough to follow up.

What should I do when a test fails?

Record the mismatch and assign the correction. I repeat the affected scenario after the fix; an unresolved failure does not count as a completed check.

Should I activate every call type at once?

I start with limited after-hours or overflow coverage. The service process calls for reviewing those results before adding more responsibility.
