What happens when the front desk is checking in a patient?

Picture your Valencia receptionist checking in a patient with forms in hand. Another call comes in. I want that patient served and that caller heard. Your staff can answer first during the hours you choose. I set the AI to receive the calls that reach the overflow route you approve.

I use your practice name in the greeting and make the AI identity clear. Your approved hours, location, and new-patient next steps belong in that conversation. So does recognizing an existing appointment. I leave cancellations with your scheduling team unless we have specifically configured that action and tested what it does.

Which dental intake questions belong on the call?

For dental intake, I start small. New patient or existing patient? Name, callback number, reason for calling, and a preferred appointment window when needed. I want the caller able to correct their number. Your front desk needs a number that works when someone sits down to return that cleaning request.

I do not need a medication history to record a cleaning request. If a patient describes a concern, I want their words preserved. No diagnosis added. You and I decide which details belong in the summary and which staff members can see them before patient inquiries enter that process.

I draw a firm line at insurance verification. The AI can repeat your approved statement about accepted plans. That does not establish coverage for a particular treatment. I send benefits, eligibility, and patient-responsibility questions to the person in your office who actually verifies them.

Can patients book while the practice is closed?

After closing, I can have the AI take a preferred day without waking your front desk. It explains when staff will review the request. I check closed days and holidays with you. If nobody works tomorrow morning, I will not put a morning callback promise in your greeting.

Booking a dental visit takes more than finding open space. I need a supported calendar connection, approved visit types, and appointment lengths. Then I test availability, conflicts, and failures. A consultation slot does not become a procedure slot just because the AI can see it sitting empty.

When the calendar cannot confirm, I want the patient told that staff still need to confirm. The saved record must agree. Your receptionist should be able to distinguish a requested time from a reserved visit without finding out from an unexpected patient standing in the lobby.

What must the AI leave to your dental team?

I will not put a dental decision in the receptionist's hands. No diagnosing pain, recommending treatment, prescribing medication, quoting procedures, or giving medical or dental advice. It cannot dismiss a concern as harmless, determine insurance coverage, or promise an immediate visit with your dentist. Those decisions belong with people.

Before activation, you and I review the emergency handoff. Who gets the transfer or alert? What triggers it? What happens when that person cannot answer? I keep the intake to your approved essentials and the explanation to the actual next step. Answering the phone does not make this an emergency service.

Your clinical lead approves the safety wording. I test that route separately from booking a cleaning. Once a call meets your urgent-response rule, I want it on that path. A patient should not have to finish an ordinary sales conversation to reach the human your practice designated.

How do we test your practice before go-live?

I start the working test with your greeting and office rules. Have a staff member request a cleaning as a new patient. Then try an existing appointment, an exact insurance-payment question, and a request for a dentist. These are rehearsal calls. I am not presenting them as patient results.

After each test, I read what your front desk would receive. Name. Corrected number. Visit preference. Confirmation status. Then I make an offered time unavailable and run it again. I leave the transfer unanswered, too. I want to hear the fallback before a patient has to hear it.

Before real calls move, I review recording, transcription, AI processing, retention, and access with your practice. Turning off audio recording does not mean all stored call data disappear. I want you to understand what the proposed setup keeps and who can reach it before patient inquiries pass through.

How will your front desk know whether this helps?

You and I give the dental request list an owner and a backup. That person records whether the patient was reached, scheduling finished, or something still needs attention. I compare those outcomes with your call records. Answering a question does not establish a new patient or a completed visit.

Bring me the calls your front desk struggles with and the scheduling rules they already use. I start at that specific gap. If routine intake passes but a special visit type fails, I keep that visit with staff while we correct the process. Your patients should not be the test.

What do dental practice owners ask before starting?

Will the AI answer before my dental receptionist?

I can keep your dental receptionist first during staffed hours. Once carrier forwarding checks out, I use the agreed overflow and closed-hours routes. You decide which practice calls reach the AI and which stay with your staff.

Can it reserve a hygiene appointment?

Only after I test the supported calendar against your approved hygiene scheduling rules. Otherwise, the AI records a preferred time for your front desk. I make it call that a request. Confirmation is still pending.

What happens when a patient describes severe tooth pain?

I use your practice-approved urgent route to a human. The AI gathers only permitted details. It neither diagnoses the pain nor suggests treatment. I also check what the patient hears if the intended staff contact cannot be reached.

Will it quote my patient's insurance payment?

No. I leave benefits, treatment coverage, and payment responsibility with your staff. The AI may use your approved information about accepted plans. I do not let that general statement sound like someone's coverage has already been verified.

What should I bring to the fit call?
