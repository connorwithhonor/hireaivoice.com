This does not replace anybody

An A I cannot be human. It cannot sit with a grieving family, read a room, or know what your program director knows. It should never try.

What it does is cover the hours nobody is there. Nights, weekends, lunch, the middle of your gala, the week your one administrator is on leave. It answers, it takes the details, it texts your team, and then a real person calls back. The human contact still happens. It just stops depending on somebody being at a desk at the moment the phone rings.

What it actually handles

Donor calls

Someone wants to give, change a recurring gift, ask where to mail a check, or ask if you take stock. It takes the details and gets them to the right person instead of a voicemail box.

Volunteer interest

The hardest moment to lose is the one where somebody decides to help. It captures the name, the number, and what they want to do, at eleven at night if that is when they called.

Program and intake routing

It finds out which program the caller needs and routes the message there. It does not assess need, and it does not decide who qualifies. That stays with your staff.

Events and registration

Gala questions, ticket questions, drop-off times, what to bring. The calls that flood in for two weeks and then stop.

Hours and directions

The questions that eat your administrator's day and never needed a person in the first place.

After-hours coverage

You keep your number and your phone provider. Forwarding turns on after hours and off again whenever you want, in seconds.

Calls we will not take

This is published on purpose, because a nonprofit deserves to know where the line is before it signs anything, not after.

We do not put an A I on a line where the caller may be in crisis. Not suicide or crisis lines. Not domestic violence hotlines. Not mental health or substance use intake. Not child protective services. Not any line where the person calling may be at risk in that moment.

  • An A I cannot recognize what a trained human recognizes in a voice
  • It cannot be accountable for a call that goes wrong
  • The research on generative systems in crisis contexts is not reassuring, and the failures have been serious

If part of your organization runs a line like that, we will happily cover your donor, volunteer and general lines and leave that one alone. We will tell you so on the first call rather than sell you the whole building.

The questions your board will ask

Does the caller know it is an A I?

Yes. The agent identifies itself as A I in the first breath of every call, before the conversation continues.

Are calls recorded?

Audio recording, AI processing, transcripts, and stored summaries are separate settings. Before activation, we explain the actual configuration, access, retention, notice, and consent process. Disabling audio recording does not mean no call data is processed.

What happens to what it hears?

The service processes caller details to handle the request and deliver the agreed staff handoff. Before activation, review the privacy terms, service providers, access controls, retention, and deletion process for your configuration.

What if it does not know an answer?

The approved call flow directs the assistant to take a message when an answer is outside its knowledge or authority. AI can make mistakes. Test program questions, eligibility limits, prices, and the human fallback before launch.

Try to break it before you trust it

Call the demo line. Tell it you run a food bank. Ask it something it should not answer and watch it refuse. That is the part worth testing.
