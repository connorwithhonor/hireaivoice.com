Answering the phone is step one.

Every business needs the phone answered around the clock. That is why we start there. But the call is not where you lose the money. You lose it in the six things that were supposed to happen after the call and never did.

Seven steps. Nothing falls out.

You do not buy these one at a time. You start with the phone, because that is the leak you can feel. The rest is what stops the same lead leaking twice.

01

Answer

Every call, day or night, weekend or holiday. Not a menu and not a robot reading options. It talks, it listens, and it works out what the caller actually needs. The call that used to go to voicemail is now a conversation.

02

Book

It puts them on your calendar while they are still interested, which is the only moment that reliably converts. No phone tag, no waiting until Monday, no calling back to find they already hired somebody else.

03

Nurture

Most people who call are not ready today. That is normal, and it is where nearly every small business loses the money. Instead of dying in a notebook, they stay warm on their own until the day they are ready, and then they come back to you.

04

Deliver

They buy. The system knows who actually bought, what they bought, and when. Almost no small business can answer that question about its own customers, which is why the next three steps never happen.

05

Reputation

The review gets asked for at the one moment they are happiest, which is right after the job went well. What comes back gets watched and handled instead of hoped for. Your reputation stops being weather and starts being something you own.

06

Refer

That same happy moment is the only good time to ask who else needs this. Asking once, by hand, when you remember, is not a strategy. Asking every satisfied customer, every time, is.

07

Reactivate

Old customers are the cheapest work you will ever get, and they are sitting in a list nobody looks at. The system watches for a real signal that somebody is back in the market, and when it sees one, you get told. Something is brewing. Go call them.

Then it starts over. The customer you woke up in step seven calls the number in step one, and the whole thing runs again without anybody remembering to do it.

Why it starts with the phone and not the software

Because you can feel a missed call. You cannot feel a customer you forgot to ask for a review, or a past client who quietly hired somebody else last spring. Those cost more, and they are invisible, which is exactly why nobody fixes them first.

Step one pays for itself and proves the thing works. Once your calls are being answered and booked, every step after it is a switch we turn on, not a new system you have to learn. You already have the contact records by then. That is the whole trick.

One person builds this, and that person is Connor. That is the reason for a founding ten and not a founding hundred.
